# Figure 7a: ESR1 is the only estrogen receptor broadly expressed across the lateral plate mesoderm

**Across all estrogen receptors expressed in the dataset, ESR1 alone is expressed throughout the lateral plate mesoderm (LPM), identifying it as the candidate estrogen receptor for the syrinx.** Data: a CellBender-corrected (CellBender; Fleming et al. 2023), intron-inclusive re-derivation of the E6/E9/E11 duck 3′ scRNA-seq.

## Panel

**(A) Estrogen-receptor expression across LPM subclusters and non-mesoderm reference lineages.** Six estrogen-receptor violins — *ESR1, ESR2, ESRRB, ESRRG\*, GPER1, RERG* (ESR1 first, manuscript order) — across the named LPM mesenchyme subclusters (Skeletogenic LPM, Ventral splanchnic [Ventral spl.], Dorsal sub-epithelial [Dorsal sub-epi.], Mesothelium, Syringeal chondrocyte, Syringeal fibroblast, Syringeal smooth muscle), stage-blocked (E06 │ E09 │ E11, implicit faceting), plus the three non-mesoderm reference lineages (Endoderm, Endothelium, Immune; pooled across stages). Violins are coloured by population, so a recurring subcluster keeps one colour across stage blocks (colour encodes population identity only). Median bar per violin; y starts at 0.
- **Key observation.** ESR1 is broadly expressed across the LPM mesenchyme at every stage and is the only estrogen receptor to do so; ESR2, ESRRB and GPER1 are near-silent, and RERG is restricted (syringeal smooth muscle and endoderm). *ESRRG\** also reads broad, but likely from intron-inclusive (pre-mRNA) counting of its long introns (see Figure S14).
- The three non-mesoderm lineages are each a single, well-separated global cluster, shown as distinct reference violins.

This panel is manuscript Figure 7a (the 8-main layout; formerly Fig 6a), replacing the cr6-era M1-M8 violin block.
